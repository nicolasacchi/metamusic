**Goal.** Two optional transports for the existing yue2 engine, both off until the operator turns them on. (1) **Bulk pods:** a large
human-approved batch runs on one short-lived RunPod pod that pulls takes from the app, renders them one after another and destroys
itself; at current list prices a take costs well under half of a serverless take (figures in the private appendix). (2) **Upload fallback:** a paid render survives an unreachable app, because
the worker can put its artifacts into a private S3-compatible bucket (R2 recommended), from which the app fetches, verifies and deletes
them. Both reuse what E2 and G1 built: the backend set, the per-backend gate, runner, reconciler and billing, the `mm.worker/2`
protocol and its capability gate. Neither adds a spend path outside `estimate_generation` / `create_generation`.

**Architecture (one paragraph).** `runpod-pod` is one more `provider.Backend` in E2's set (engine `yue2`, routed only when an estimate
names it in `options.backend`). Its `Submit` puts the take into an in-memory hand-out queue; a pod-lifecycle loop creates **one pod per
batch** through RunPod's REST v2 pod API with a scoped pod key that is read from a mode-600 file at every call. The pod runs the
ordinary yue2 image with `MODE=agent`: it long-polls `POST /worker/v1/pods/{id}/poll` (a signed, pod-bound capability URL, no inbound
ports), gets one `mm.worker/1` or `/2` document at a time, runs the same `process()` as serverless, uploads through the existing signed
upload URLs and reports `done`. Money is M3's: a `take` reserve per job at create, plus a `pod_overhead` reserve per pod (boot, idle,
slack, kill slack, overrun) taken when the pod is created; the pod's lifetime settles through a pod meter at batch finish and anything
above the reserves settles as `excess` with a trip. The pod dies by its own hand (absolute deadline, a dead-man switch on failed polls,
or a `stop` from the server), by the server's DELETE (idle, deadline, orphan, trip) or, as last resort, by the operator. The upload
fallback is a generic SigV4 client (`internal/objstore`), presigned `PUT` URLs minted with the primary ones (keys are the upload UUID),
a sticky worker switch after two minutes of transport failures, and a server rescue that runs before any time-bound settlement.

  no MCP entry, no OpenAPI path.
- **D-BULK-5 Hand-out queue in memory.** `Submit` records an assignment row and keeps the `TakeSpec` (with its URLs) in memory; URLs
  are never persisted. After a restart an unhanded assignment answers `Cancelled` without execution, pinned as never-ran for this
  backend, and the runner requeues it.
- **D-BULK-6 Money.** Per job the M3 `take` reserve at the pod price (create, unchanged); per pod a `pod_overhead` reserve of
  `boot + idle + slack + kill slack + overrun` seconds taken **when the pod is created**, under the batch ceiling and the caps; ceiling =
  max + one take's retry + one re-creation. The batch deadline is absolute and includes one retry; a pod billed until the overrun trip
  costs at most the ceiling (property test). The lifetime settles at batch finish through a pod meter; the rest settles as `excess` with
  trip `pod_excess`.
- **D-BULK-7 Lifetime** is measured conservatively: `create_sent_at` to the first observation that the pod is gone, rounded up to the
  minute. Execution per take is measured by the server (`done_at − handed_at`); the agent's figure is diagnostic only.
- **D-BULK-8 Shape.** One pod per batch, one take at a time (`workers_max = 1`), one GPU type per create from an ordered list priced at
  its maximum, SECURE cloud only, at most one re-creation, a restarted container at most once. Never `queue`, `templateId`, `mounts`,
  ports, SSH or Jupyter; no network volume.
- **D-BULK-9 Trips** (new reasons on the pod backend): `pod_overrun`, `pod_deadman`, `pod_unknown`, `pod_price`, `pod_access`,
  `pod_excess`, `pod_create_invalid`. The action is M3's (`actOnTrips`): cancel live attempts, purge the hand-out queue, ScaleToZero =
  DELETE every live `mm-*` pod.
- **D-BULK-10 No new notification channel.** Pod alerts show in `get_provider_status`, `metamusic runpod status` and the Jobs page.
  Bulk windows are attended; the existing ntfy calls stay but nothing relies on them.
- **D-BULK-11 Bulk windows** are operator-triggered: place the key file, arm `runpod-pod`. The backend disarms itself after
  `MM_POD_WINDOW_MAX` (default 8 h) from arming, or after 30 minutes with no pod batch in progress. The runbook deletes the key file after
  each window; `scripts/deploy-preflight.sh` refuses while a pod is live.
- **D-BULK-12 Safety gate.** Ordinary pod estimates and pod creation need the latest canary row in `pod_safety_log` for the configured
  image digest to be `verified` (disabled reason `pod_safety_unverified`); arming needs a probe row `ok` (or `too_broad` plus the
  flag). The self-termination canary itself is the only pod batch allowed before that.
- **D-BULK-13 Who may.** Pod estimates are refused for every principal except the Owner acting as human (agents, tokens including a
  mandate or night-shift token, devices: 403 `HUMAN_ONLY`; Editors and Listeners: 403 `OWNER_ONLY`), with one exception: the canary
  estimate, which a token may make and which always waits for the Owner's approval on `/human`. Takes `MM_POD_MIN_TAKES` (30) ..
  `MM_POD_MAX_TAKES` (100); kind `generate` only; `needs` gain `agent`.
- **D-BULK-14 Operations.** One new op, `list_pods` (OwnerOnly, `read`, MCP generic, UI `/jobs`). Stopping a pod batch is
  `cancel_batch` (never spends, never re-creates). Status lives in `get_provider_status` (OwnerOnly, not a first-class tool).
- **D-BULK-15 Agent boot order.** Poll first (the dead-man is fed from the first second, and a canary costs only the image pull), then
  fetch the pinned 3B weights with per-file sha256 checks (no token), then load. Terminator: DELETE → action `terminate` → action `stop`
  → `os._exit(3)`; the RunPod-injected pod-scoped key, `User-Agent: metamusic-worker/<v>`, nothing logged but the step.
- **D-BULK-16 Pod image** is `MM_POD_IMAGE`, a `ref@sha256:` reference; the runbook sets it to the yue2 image the serverless endpoint
  runs. G1's capability gate binds the pod backend's caps to that digest; a new digest closes them and needs a new canary.
- **D-BULK-17 Upload fallback provider: Cloudflare R2** (D-G1-18 confirmed; reasons in C398.2). Backblaze B2's S3 API stays a
  configuration change, because the client is generic SigV4.
- **D-BULK-18 Fallback flow.** Fallback URLs are minted only when the attempt goes out as `/2` with capability `fallback` open; key =
  prefix + upload UUID; the worker switches after `fallback_after_s` (default 120) of transport failures, per attempt, sticky, never on
  an answered 4xx; the server finds the objects by `HEAD` (G1's `/2` output is unchanged), verifies size and sha256, records the upload
  `via = 'fallback'` and lets the normal result path complete the take; objects are deleted once the attempt is terminal; the 7-day
  lifecycle rule is the backstop.
- **D-BULK-19 `r2.env`** is read once at startup (rotation = restart), mode 0600, never in compose `environment`/`env_file`.
- **D-BULK-20 Drill.** The R2 drill is an attempt-scoped, time-bounded, CLI-only `upload_drills` row: the primary upload route answers 503
  for that attempt; the outage-past-retention case is proven in CI only.

### C395. Poll protocol and hand-out (`pods/poll.go`; mounted at `/worker/v1/pods/` by `internal/httpserver/server.go`)

1. **Link.** `POST /worker/v1/pods/{pod_id}/poll?exp=<unix>&sig=<base64url_nopad(HMAC-SHA256(PodPollKey, "v1|pod|" + pod_id + "|" + exp))>`;
   `exp = deadline_at + 600`. Checks in order: method (405), path shape (404), MAC (403 `POD_LINK_INVALID`), expiry (403 `POD_LINK_EXPIRED`),
   body ≤ 16 KiB (413), strict JSON (400), pod row missing (403 `POD_LINK_INVALID`), pod `terminating | terminated | orphaned` or batch
   cancelled ⇒ 200 `{"action":"stop"}`. Problem bodies are written by the handler. Access log: method, path without query, status, bytes.
2. **Request** (`worker/src/mmworker/schema/mm-pod-poll-1.request.schema.json`, `additionalProperties: false`):
   ```json
   {"state":"booting|idle|busy","agent":{"version":"…","build":"…"},
    "boot":{"gpu_name":"…","cuda":"…","weights_ready":true,"load_s":41}|null,
    "attempt_id":"0190…"|null,
    "done":{"attempt_id":"0190…","status":"ok|error","execution_ms":61234,"error_code":null}|null}
   ```
   **Response** (`…poll-1.response.schema.json`): `{"action":"wait|take|stop","wait_s":20,"take":<an mm.worker/1 or /2 input>|null,
   "cancel":["<attempt id>"],"deadline_unix":1760018552}`.
3. **Observations only.** The handler sets `last_poll_at`, `first_poll_at` (once), `last_reported`, `boot_json`, and on `done` the
   assignment's `done_at`, `status`, `agent_execution_ms`, `error_code` and the server-measured `execution_ms = done_at − handed_at` (once).
   Pod and job states are moved only by the lifecycle loop and the runner (single writer, D-M3-3); the handler nudges the loop through an
   in-process channel.

## Rollout and risks

1. **A pod that never dies** (highest). Layers: the self-termination canary gates every ordinary pod; absolute deadline in the pod env (a
   restart cannot extend it); dead-man on failed polls; server `stop` and DELETE (idle, deadline, orphan, trips); name sweep; overrun and
   dead-man trips; attended windows with auto-disarm; preflight refuses deploys with a live pod; the money is reserved up to the overrun
   trip and anything beyond is settled and visible. Residual: key removed and agent broken at the same time ⇒ the operator deletes it in
   the console (runbook), bounded by the account balance.
2. **Pod key on the server** (accepted, D-BULK-OP-6): scoped key, read per call from a mode-600 file, never in the environment, deleted
   after each window, `too_broad` detection, redaction and gitleaks rules.
3. **The poll URL is visible** to anyone who can read the RunPod account's pod configuration: it is bound to one pod and its deadline,
   hands out only that pod's takes and records observations only.
4. **Pod API drift.** The live spec added `queue`, a 202 reply and `tags` since the snapshot: refreshed snapshot, a body validated against
   it, banned-field tests, the weekly drift job.
5. **Money model.** Property tests on the quote and the spend profile, per-backend billing reconciliation from `/v2/billing/pods`, `excess`
   plus trip. Unknown: whether image pulls are billed (boot allowance 1 200 s assumes so; the canary and pilot measure).
6. **Capacity.** One type per create, an ordered list priced at its maximum, retry every 5 minutes, the M3 24 h bound.
7. **R2 privacy** (accepted): UUID keys, private EU bucket, deletion when the attempt is terminal, 7-day lifecycle backstop,
   `objects_pending` visible. **R2 integrity:** bounded `GET`, size and sha256 against the output, strict decode with id checks.
8. **Server first.** Neither half changes G1's `/2` output; the server halves merge and deploy before the worker wave; capabilities open
   last, right before their canary.
9. **Fallback hiding an outage:** every fallback receipt is a job event with `via`, and `last_used_at` is in the status.
10. **Hot spots:** the serial edits of A6, one merge at a time, `make regen` after every rebase.
11. **MCP growth:** about 300 bytes on `estimate_generation`'s output; `list_pods` is generic.
